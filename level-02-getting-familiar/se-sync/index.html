SE-Sync

Rosen 2019 · Paper

One-line summary — First certifiably correct algorithm for pose graph optimization: an SDP relaxation solved via Riemannian optimization that recovers the globally optimal solution with a proof of optimality (arXiv 2016, IJRR 2019).

Problem

SE(d)SE(d)SE(d) synchronization — estimate nnn unknown poses x1,…,xn∈SE(d)x_1,\dots,x_n \in SE(d)x1​,…,xn​∈SE(d) from noisy measurements of mmm of their pairwise relative transforms xij=xi−1xjx_{ij} = x_i^{-1}x_jxij​=xi−1​xj​ — is the standard SLAM backend problem (pose-graph SLAM, camera pose estimation). This is a high-dimensional non-convex nonlinear program, computationally hard in general: local solvers (g2o, GTSAM, Ceres) can silently converge to local minima far from the true solution, with no way to know whether a returned answer is globally optimal — unacceptable for safety-critical autonomy.

Method & architecture

  • Eliminate translations. For fixed rotations the problem is an unconstrained quadratic in ttt, solved in closed form via a generalized Schur complement. This reduces the MLE to pure rotation synchronization, pMLE∗=min⁡R∈SO(d)ntr⁡(Q~RTR)p^{*}_{\mathrm{MLE}} = \min_{R \in SO(d)^n} \operatorname{tr}(\tilde{Q} R^{\mathsf{T}} R)pMLE∗​=minR∈SO(d)n​tr(Q~​RTR), where the data matrix Q~=L(G~ρ)+T~TΩ1/2Π Ω1/2T~\tilde{Q} = L(\tilde{G}^{\rho}) + \tilde{T}^{\mathsf{T}} \Omega^{1/2} \Pi\, \Omega^{1/2} \tilde{T}Q~​=L(G~ρ)+T~TΩ1/2ΠΩ1/2T~ combines the rotation connection Laplacian L(G~ρ)L(\tilde{G}^{\rho})L(G~ρ) with a translational data term; Π\PiΠ (an orthogonal projection) admits a sparse decomposition via a thin LQ factorization of the weighted incidence matrix, so products with Q~\tilde{Q}Q~​ never form a dense matrix. Optimal translations are recovered afterwards as t∗=−vec⁡(R∗V~TL(Wτ)†)t^{*} = -\operatorname{vec}\big(R^{*}\tilde{V}^{\mathsf{T}} L(W^{\tau})^{\dagger}\big)t∗=−vec(R∗V~TL(Wτ)†).

  • Riemannian staircase. Instead of interior-point SDP solvers (intractable beyond a few thousand variables), SE-Sync uses the Burer–Monteiro factorization Z=YTYZ = Y^{\mathsf{T}}YZ=YTY with Y∈Rr×dnY \in \mathbb{R}^{r\times dn}Y∈Rr×dn, r≪dnr \ll dnr≪dn; the block constraints then say each YiY_iYi​ is an orthonormal frame, giving an unconstrained problem on a product of Stiefel manifolds:

pSDPLR∗=min⁡Y∈St(d,r)ntr⁡(Q~ YTY).p^{*}_{\mathrm{SDPLR}} = \min_{Y \in \mathrm{St}(d,r)^n} \operatorname{tr}(\tilde{Q}\, Y^{\mathsf{T}} Y).pSDPLR∗​=Y∈St(d,r)nmin​tr(Q~​YTY).

Proposition 2 (after Boumal et al.): any rank-deficient second-order critical point of this problem is a global minimizer and yields the SDP solution — so one climbs the rank hierarchy (“Riemannian staircase”) until a rank-deficient critical point appears.

  • Fast second-order local search. On the manifold, ∇F(Y)=2YQ~\nabla F(Y) = 2Y\tilde{Q}∇F(Y)=2YQ~​ and Hessian-vector products are projections of ambient derivatives (grad⁡F(Y)=Proj⁡Y∇F(Y)\operatorname{grad} F(Y) = \operatorname{Proj}_Y \nabla F(Y)gradF(Y)=ProjY​∇F(Y)), all computed with sparse matrix products and triangular solves; a truncated-Newton Riemannian trust-region (RTR) method finds high-precision critical points.
  • Rounding. A rank-ddd thin SVD of Y∗Y^{*}Y∗ gives R^=ΞdVdT\hat{R} = \Xi_d V_d^{\mathsf{T}}R^=Ξd​VdT​; flip orientation if most blocks have negative determinant, then project each block to the nearest rotation matrix — exact when the relaxation is tight, a feasible approximation otherwise.

Results

(MATLAB implementation on Manopt, staircase fixed at r=5r=5r=5, initialized from a random point on St(3,5)n\mathrm{St}(3,5)^nSt(3,5)n; baselines: Gauss–Newton with odometric init, GN with chordal init, and GN-chordal plus a posteriori verification.)

  • Simulated cube worlds (s3s^3s3 lattice, loop-closure probability pLCp_{LC}pLC​, noise σT\sigma_TσT​, σR\sigma_RσR​; 30 runs per setting): SE-Sync converges to a certifiably globally optimal solution from random initialization in time comparable to — in these tests often faster than — GN with state-of-the-art chordal initialization, and much faster than GN + separate verification. The exception is the high-rotational-noise regime, where the relaxation’s exactness breaks down.
  • Large-scale real/standard 3D SLAM datasets — sphere (2500 nodes/4949 edges), sphere-a (2200/8647), torus (5000/9048), cube (8000/22236), garage (1661/6275), cubicle (5750/16869): SE-Sync attains the certified global optimum on all of them (e.g. objective 1.249×1061.249\times 10^{6}1.249×106 on sphere-a vs 3.041×1063.041\times 10^{6}3.041×106 for odometry-initialized GN), in 3.6–203 s, confirming the relaxation stays exact on challenging real-world instances.
  • Per the abstract, global optimality is recovered under noise “up to an order of magnitude greater than that typically encountered in robotics applications,” at a cost scaling comparably with direct Newton-type local search.

Why it matters for SLAM

SE-Sync answered a foundational question: despite PGO’s non-convexity, the instances arising in practical SLAM are globally solvable, and you can know when you have the global optimum. This launched the certifiable-perception research program (TEASER++ for registration, QUASAR for rotation search) and gave SLAM backends a verification tool — e.g., checking whether a local solver’s answer is actually optimal after potentially corrupted loop closures.
