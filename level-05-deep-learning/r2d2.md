# R2D2

> Revaud 2019 · [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.06195)

**One-line summary** — Jointly learns explicit *repeatability* and *reliability* maps alongside dense descriptors, so keypoints are only detected where they can actually be matched.

## Problem

Classical local features follow a detect-then-describe paradigm: hand-crafted detectors find repeatable keypoints, then a separate descriptor represents them. Learned methods had caught up by training repeatable saliency maps and metric-learning descriptors at those locations — but R2D2's authors argue this conflates two different properties.

Salient regions are not necessarily *discriminative*: a corner on a repetitive facade is detected consistently yet matches ambiguously. Descriptors should only be trusted (and keypoints only detected) where matching can be performed with high confidence.

## Key ideas

- **Repeatable is not reliable**: The core insight — salient regions (high-gradient corners, repetitive textures) are not necessarily *discriminable*. Detecting features at unreliable locations poisons matching precision.
- **Three dense outputs from one network**: A descriptor map $\mathbf{D}$, a repeatability map $\mathcal{S}$ (will this point be detected consistently across viewpoints?), and a reliability map $\mathcal{R}$ (is its descriptor discriminative enough to match correctly?). Detection and description are learned jointly with an explicit predictor of descriptor discriminativeness.
- **Task-specific losses**: Repeatability is trained self-supervised with cosine similarity of response maps across image pairs related by known transformations; reliability is trained by directly maximizing the Average Precision of descriptor matching.
- **Ranking, not distance margins**: The AP loss treats descriptor matching as a *ranking* problem and optimizes the retrieval metric actually used at test time, rather than a surrogate contrastive/triplet margin — matching quality is the training objective, not a proxy for it.
- **Filtered keypoint selection**: At test time, keep only locations where both $\mathcal{S}(x) > \tau_s$ and $\mathcal{R}(x) > \tau_r$ — rejecting salient-but-ambiguous points such as repeated windows on a facade, and yielding sparse keypoints that are simultaneously repeatable and reliable.
- **Quality over quantity**: Fewer, better keypoints improve matching precision, which matters more than raw keypoint count for pose estimation and localization.

## Results & impact

- Outperformed state-of-the-art detectors and descriptors (including SuperPoint and D2-Net) on the HPatches benchmark.
- Set a record on the then-recently released Aachen Day-Night visual localization benchmark — evidence the reliability filtering pays off exactly where appearance change makes matching hard.
- Published at NeurIPS 2019 (Naver Labs Europe); the explicit separation of repeatability from reliability became a standard consideration in later detector/descriptor designs (e.g., DeDoDe's fully decoupled detection and description).

## Why it matters for SLAM

Wrong matches are the dominant failure mode of feature-based SLAM under repetitive structure (corridors, facades, vegetation). R2D2 established the principle of *reliability-aware* keypoint selection — quality over quantity — which carried into later detector/descriptor designs and into how modern pipelines score and filter correspondences for localization and 3D reconstruction.
