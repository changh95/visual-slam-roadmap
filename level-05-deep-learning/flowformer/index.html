FlowFormer

Huang 2022 · Paper

One-line summary — First Transformer architecture built around the 4D cost volume for optical flow: it tokenizes the cost volume, encodes the tokens into a latent “cost memory” with alternate-group attention, and decodes flow recurrently with dynamic positional cost queries.

Problem

Optical flow estimates a per-pixel displacement field f:R2→R2\mathbf{f}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}f:R2→R2 mapping each source-image location x\mathbf{x}x to its target-image correspondence p=x+f(x)\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x})p=x+f(x). RAFT builds an H×W×H×WH \times W \times H \times WH×W×H×W 4D cost volume of all-pairs similarities but retrieves costs only from local windows, which struggles with large displacements and occlusions. Transformers offer global reasoning, but naive self-attention over thousands of cost-volume tokens is computationally unbearable — Perceiver IO attends over raw pixels instead and needs roughly 80×80\times80× more training examples. FlowFormer asks how to keep the compact cost volume and gain Transformer-style global aggregation.

Method & architecture

Three stages: build a 4D cost volume, encode it into cost memory, decode flow recurrently.

  • Cost volume: the first two stages of an ImageNet-pretrained Twins-SVT backbone extract H×W×DfH \times W \times D_fH×W×Df​ features (Df=256D_f{=}256Df​=256, 1/8 resolution); dot-product similarities between all source/target feature pairs form the H×W×H×WH \times W \times H \times WH×W×H×W volume, viewed as one 2D cost map Mx∈RH×W\mathbf{M_x} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W}Mx​∈RH×W per source pixel x\mathbf{x}x.
  • Two-step tokenization: each cost map is patchified by three stride-2 convolutions into 8×88{\times}88×8-patch features Fx\mathbf{F_x}Fx​ (Dp=64D_p{=}64Dp​=64 channels), then summarized into KKK latent tokens by learned codewords C∈RK×D\mathbf{C}\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times D}C∈RK×D (shared across pixels, trained by backprop):

Kx=Conv1×1(Concat(Fx,PE)),Vx=Conv1×1(Concat(Fx,PE)),Tx=Attention(C,Kx,Vx)\mathbf{K_x}=\mathrm{Conv}_{1\times 1}(\mathrm{Concat}(\mathbf{F_x},\mathrm{PE})),\quad \mathbf{V_x}=\mathrm{Conv}_{1\times 1}(\mathrm{Concat}(\mathbf{F_x},\mathrm{PE})),\quad \mathbf{T_x}=\mathrm{Attention}(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{K_x},\mathbf{V_x})Kx​=Conv1×1​(Concat(Fx​,PE)),Vx​=Conv1×1​(Concat(Fx​,PE)),Tx​=Attention(C,Kx​,Vx​)

turning the 4D volume into an H×W×KH \times W \times KH×W×K token grid (K×D≪H×WK \times D \ll H \times WK×D≪H×W; 8 tokens of dimension 128 in the final model).

  • Alternate-Group Transformer (AGT) layers (3 in the final model) alternate two orthogonal groupings: intra-cost-map self-attention over each pixel’s KKK tokens, Tx=FFN(SelfAttention(Tx(1),…,Tx(K)))\mathbf{T_x}=\mathrm{FFN}(\mathrm{SelfAttention}(\mathbf{T_x}(1),\dots,\mathbf{T_x}(K)))Tx​=FFN(SelfAttention(Tx​(1),…,Tx​(K))), and inter-cost-map spatially-separable self-attention (from Twins) over each of the KKK groups of H×WH \times WH×W tokens, Ti=FFN(SSSelfAttention(Ti))\mathbf{T}_i=\mathrm{FFN}(\mathrm{SSSelfAttention}(\mathbf{T}_i))Ti​=FFN(SSSelfAttention(Ti​)), with source-image context features injected into queries/keys so visually similar pixels get coherent flows. The output tokens are the cost memory.
  • Recurrent decoder with dynamic positional cost queries: at each iteration the current flow gives p=x+f(x)\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x})p=x+f(x); a local 9×99{\times}99×9 cost patch qx=Crop9×9(Mx,p)\mathbf{q_x}=\mathrm{Crop}_{9\times 9}(\mathbf{M_x},\mathbf{p})qx​=Crop9×9​(Mx​,p) builds the query Qx=FFN(FFN(qx)+PE(p))\mathbf{Q_x}=\mathrm{FFN}(\mathrm{FFN}(\mathbf{q_x})+\mathrm{PE}(\mathbf{p}))Qx​=FFN(FFN(qx​)+PE(p)), which cross-attends into the cost memory, cx=Attention(Qx,Kx,Vx)\mathbf{c_x}=\mathrm{Attention}(\mathbf{Q_x},\mathbf{K_x},\mathbf{V_x})cx​=Attention(Qx​,Kx​,Vx​) (keys/values computed once and reused). A ConvGRU regresses the residual

Δf(x)=ConvGRU(Concat(cx,qx), tx, f(x))\Delta\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x})=\mathrm{ConvGRU}(\mathrm{Concat}(\mathbf{c_x},\mathbf{q_x}),\,\mathbf{t_x},\,\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}))Δf(x)=ConvGRU(Concat(cx​,qx​),tx​,f(x))

and flows are convex-upsampled to full resolution, supervised at every iteration with increasing weights.

Results

  • Sintel test (C+T+S+K+H): 1.159 AEPE clean / 2.088 final — 16.5% and 15.5% error reduction from the best published results (1.388 and 2.47, GMA with warm-start), ranking 1st on both passes without warm-start; vs. GMA without warm-start, 17.2%/27.5% reduction.
  • Generalization (C+T only): 1.01 / 2.40 AEPE on Sintel train clean/final and 4.09 F1-epe / 14.72 F1-all on KITTI-2015 train — vs. GMA, 22.3% and 12.4% lower error on Sintel clean/final and 13.9% lower KITTI F1-all; the 1.01 clean AEPE beats the best published result (1.29) by 21.7%.
  • KITTI-2015 test: 4.68 F1-all after KITTI finetuning, ranking 2nd (S-Flow’s 4.64 is 0.85% lower, but S-Flow is 31.6%/22.5% worse on Sintel clean/final).
  • First validation that an ImageNet-pretrained transformer backbone benefits optical flow estimation.

Why it matters for SLAM

Dense optical flow is the correspondence engine inside modern learned SLAM front-ends (DROID-SLAM, DPVO descendants), and FlowFormer demonstrated that global attention over matching costs resolves the long-range, ambiguous correspondences that matter most for wide-baseline motion — precisely the hard cases (large displacement, occlusion) its cost memory targets. It established the Transformer side of today’s trade-off — Transformer accuracy (FlowFormer) versus convolutional efficiency (SEA-RAFT) — that SLAM engineers weigh when picking a flow backbone.
